# GSB Standards — Edition 2025

**Global Standards Board (GSB)**
Published 30 June 2024. Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025.
This edition supersedes the GSB Standards, Edition 2021.

## Preface

The GSB Standards set out requirements and recommendations for the construction of
composites and the presentation of investment performance by firms that claim
compliance. Provisions marked **A** are requirements and use the word *must*.
Provisions marked **B** are recommendations and use the word *should*. Section 1
contains the definitions on which all other provisions depend; a term defined in
Section 1 carries that meaning wherever it appears.

This edition introduces new definitions (1.D.4 Benchmark; 1.D.10 Measurement period),
revises the definition of a discretionary portfolio, and adds two provisions to
Section 3. **Provisions in Sections 1 and 3 have been renumbered accordingly.** Firms
operating in jurisdictions whose sponsors have announced a later adoption date must
continue to apply Edition 2021 until that date.

## Section 1 — Definitions

**1.D.1 Composite.** A composite is an aggregation of one or more portfolios managed
according to a similar investment mandate, objective, or strategy.

**1.D.2 Firm.** A firm is the entity defined for the purpose of claiming compliance
with the GSB Standards, comprising all portfolios managed under its investment
authority.

**1.D.3 Portfolio.** A portfolio is an individually managed group of investments, which
may be a segregated account or a pooled fund.

**1.D.4 Benchmark.** A point of reference against which the performance of a composite
is compared, selected to reflect the composite's investment mandate, objective, or
strategy.

**1.D.5 Discretionary portfolio.** A portfolio is discretionary when the firm has
sufficient authority to implement its intended investment strategy. Client-imposed
restrictions render a portfolio non-discretionary only where they materially impede
implementation of the strategy. Firms must document the basis for each such
determination.

**1.D.6 Fee-paying portfolio.** A fee-paying portfolio is one for which the firm
receives compensation for investment management services, whether or not the fee is
paid directly from the portfolio.

**1.D.7 Gross-of-fees return.** The return on investments reduced by transaction costs
only.

**1.D.8 Net-of-fees return.** The gross-of-fees return reduced by investment
management fees.

**1.D.9 External cash flow.** Capital, in cash or investments, that enters or exits a
portfolio at the direction of the client.

**1.D.10 Measurement period.** The calendar month, unless the firm's documented policy
for a composite specifies a shorter period.

**1.D.11 Significant cash flow.** A significant cash flow is a client-directed external
cash flow of a size, defined in the firm's documented policy for the composite, that
would temporarily prevent the firm from implementing the composite strategy.

## Section 2 — Input Data

**2.A.1** Portfolios must be valued in accordance with the definition of fair value and
the firm's documented valuation policy.

**2.A.2** Trade-date accounting must be used for all periods.

## Section 3 — Composite Construction

**3.A.1** All actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios must be included in at least
one composite.

**3.A.2** Non-fee-paying discretionary portfolios may be included in a composite. Where
included, the firm must disclose the percentage of composite assets represented by
non-fee-paying portfolios as of each annual period end.

**3.A.3** Firms must make a description of any composite available to any prospective
client on request.

**3.A.4** Firms must document the definition of each composite in writing, including
the criteria for inclusion and exclusion of portfolios.

**3.A.5** Terminated portfolios must be included in the historical performance of the
composite through the last full measurement period under management.

**3.A.6** New portfolios must be included in a composite on a timely and consistent
basis, and no later than the beginning of the first full calendar month after the
portfolio comes under management. Firms must document the inclusion date applied for
each composite.

**3.A.7** If a firm sets a minimum asset level for portfolios to be included in a
composite, the firm must not include portfolios below that minimum. Any such minimum
must be documented and applied consistently.

**3.B.1** Firms should not apply changes to a composite minimum retroactively.
